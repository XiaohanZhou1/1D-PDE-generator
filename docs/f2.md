# F2 spatially nonuniform time-periodic orbit

F2 is a calibration of the temporal Floquet interface for a full-line PDE in a spatially periodic medium. Take \(f(x)=2+\cos x\), \(e(t)=(\cos t,\sin t)^T\), \(D=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\) and \(J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\). The equation
\[
X_t=D(X_{xx}-f''X/f)+JX+(f^2-|X|^2)X
\]
has exact solution \(X_*(x,t)=f(x)e(t)\). The diffusion term cancels on \(X_*\), its length is \(f\), and its time derivative is \(JX_*\). The amplitude varies from 1 to 3 over the spatial period. The coefficients of the physical linearisation vary in both space and time. The unequal diffusivities mean that the rotating temporal coordinate frame changes the diffusion matrix. The multiplication operators couple spatial Fourier modes, so integrating independent \(2\times2\) problems as in F1 would omit these couplings.

## Generator and solver exchange

The versioned instance records the exact PDE template, phase-selected orbit, spatial period \(L=2\pi\), temporal period \(T=2\pi\), full-line perturbation space, periodic fiber domain, Bloch convention, direction of time propagation and neutral-mode policy. The workflow can read the generated instance JSON and stores a hash of the input bytes. Canonical validation rejects a change in the orbit or stability problem. The numerical backend builds the spatial fiber with the convention
\[
v(x,t)=e^{i\xi x}p(x,t),\qquad
p(x+2\pi,t)=p(x,t),\qquad \xi\in[-1/2,1/2].
\]
Within a fiber, it integrates the full coupled system on Fourier modes \(-N,\ldots,N\) over \(0\le t\le T\). The resulting matrix maps an initial periodic envelope to its envelope one period later. The calculation uses the orbit period \(T\), even though the particular linearisation also has period \(T/2\). The instability query uses temporal multiplier modulus greater than one. [Floquet theory for periodic PDEs](https://doi.org/10.1070/IM1983v021n01ABEH001642)

## Continuum proof

Write \(Q_\xi=\partial_x+i\xi\). Linearising about the orbit gives the operator
\[
L_\xi(t)=D(Q_\xi^2-f''/f)+J-2f^2 e(t)e(t)^T
\]
on periodic \(H^2\). The diffusion matrix is strictly positive and the other terms are bounded and smooth, so each fiber has a well-defined parabolic evolution on periodic \(L^2\). On the full line, the spatial Bloch transform applies to the evolution because the spatial coefficients are periodic at each time. The following estimates hold uniformly at all Bloch parameters.

For a scalar periodic test function \(p\), the ground-state transform and integration by parts give
\[
-\langle p,(Q_\xi^2-f''/f)p\rangle=\int_0^{2\pi} f^2 |Q_\xi(p/f)|^2\,dx.
\]
Since \(1\le f\le3\) and \(\min_{n\in\mathbb Z}|n+\xi|=|\xi|\) on the chosen Brillouin zone, the right hand side is at least \(\xi^2\|p\|_2^2/9\). Apply this to both components with the diffusivity factors, use skew-symmetry of \(J\), and take the real part of the energy identity. Then
\[
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|p\|_2^2
\le -\frac{\xi^2}{9}\|p\|_2^2 -2\|f e(t)^T p\|_2^2.
\]
It follows that \(\|M_\xi\|\le e^{-2\pi\xi^2/9}\) for the continuum monodromy. For every \(|\mu|>1\), the geometric resolvent series is uniformly bounded by \(1/(|\mu|-1)\). This excludes spectrum outside the unit disk for the full-line direct integral. The estimate includes all spatial Fourier modes and all intermediate Bloch parameters.

At \(\xi=0\), \(p=f(x)Je(t)\) is the time derivative of the orbit and gives multiplier 1. A unit-modulus eigenvector would have to preserve its norm for an entire period. Equality in the exact energy identity then implies \(\partial_x(p/f)=0\) and \(e(t)^Tp=0\) throughout the period. Substitution forces \(p=\alpha fJe(t)\) with a constant scalar \(\alpha\). The zero fiber monodromy is compact by parabolic smoothing, and its power bound excludes a Jordan chain at 1. Thus the neutral multiplier is simple on the cell. Its eigenfunction is spatially periodic and the full-line perturbation space is \(L^2(\mathbb R)\). The bound permits modes arbitrarily close to neutral as \(\xi\to0\) and gives no uniform exponential decay rate on the line.

## Numerical check and scope

The run refines the Fourier cutoff from 4 to 7 to 10, then tightens the temporal integration tolerance at cutoff 10. It checks a phase-vector residual at zero, spectral radii, continuum norm bounds on sampled fibers and changes under refinement. These checks verify the implementation. The full-zone certificate comes from the energy estimate.

This first interface supports the reviewed explicit spatially periodic orbit and a dense finite-dimensional monodromy on small Fourier projections. General numerically continued orbits, spatially localized oscillations and arbitrary coefficient data require further implementation. The spatial heterogeneity was chosen to give an exact orbit and a continuum certificate. It is a calibration example and remains on hold for proof-training admission. The bundled Jim solver does not expose a temporal-monodromy entry point. For integration, the piece to agree with Jim is the handoff of the phase-selected orbit and the action of the Bloch evolution on perturbations.
